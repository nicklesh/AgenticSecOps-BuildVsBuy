One convincing email is often the entire initial-access step in a
breach. Attackers rotate lure themes faster than most companies
refresh annual training — which means awareness that isn't tested
regularly decays quietly, until it's tested for real.

Module 7: phishing simulation and awareness. The one module in this
series with a dimension none of the others have — this isn't just an
engineering decision, it's an HR one.

→ An agent generates varied lure templates — never reused verbatim,
since repetition is exactly what makes a simulation easy to spot and a
real attack easy to miss.

→ GoPhish (free, OSS) handles the actual sending, tracking, and landing
pages.

→ Repeat clickers get enrolled in targeted training. Framed as support,
never discipline, never a public list. That framing isn't a nice-to-
have — punitive phishing programs measurably backfire, because people
stop reporting suspicious emails once they're afraid of the
consequences.

The build is genuinely small — about $5-8K, a week or two of
engineering. The real gating factor isn't code, it's whether HR has
bandwidth to own the program responsibly, with sign-off before anything
launches and periodic review as it continues.

Verdict: build this cheaply at almost any stage. Buy the training
*content library* specifically once you're past ~100 employees — at
that scale, keeping the lure library genuinely fresh becomes a content
problem, not an engineering one.
